The Patterns

Visibility

Ten sermons, and the week settled at 51 — partly cloudy, with the widest spread in a month. Scores ran from 30 to 70, where last week they huddled between 38 and 56. Four sermons sat overcast, four held the middle, and two broke upward into Mostly clear — the first time in four weeks that any sermon cleared the middle band. The ceiling lifted this week, and the floor dropped.

The Governing Pattern

The week's architecture, though, did not change. Proclamation averaged 23 out of 40 and Governance averaged 16 out of 40. Grace was said — more of it than last week — and it still was not what the sermons ran on. When each message reached its turning points, the weight went where it has gone all month: onto the listener's next move. One sermon put the acquittal of the cross into a single clean sentence, then handed the room a self-examination to pass. Another canceled the debt early and priced the breakthrough late, at the cost of the listener's next act of surrender.

Warm but Ungrounded

Assurance led the board again at 6.6 out of 10 — believers were told they are held, kept, and not walking alone, and that instinct deserves honor. But the grounding question is where the week divided. The two clearest sermons anchored that safety in what Christ finished. Most of the rest anchored it in presence and affection — God with you, God for you, God near you — which comforts on Sunday and cannot answer Tuesday's accusation. Nearness without a verdict is company, not security.

The Bottom of the Board

Two items sat lowest, and together they explain the week. The Problem came in at 3.8 out of 10: again and again the sermon's stated problem was digital habits, discouragement, appetites, belonging, a battle to fight — real concerns, none of them the problem the cross exists to solve. And Load-Bearing came in at 3.2 out of 10, the weakest item of the week: delete the grace statements from nine of these ten sermons and the argument keeps standing. A sermon built to solve a different problem will always be able to survive the loss of its gospel.

The Bright Spot

The two sermons that reached Mostly clear earned it. Both put genuine atonement content near the load-bearing joints of the message — one built a whole movement on the courtroom being adjourned, the other pressed a table-fellowship gospel with real warmth and real textual care. What kept them short of clear skies is the same narrow thing the instrument always asks: their structures still ran on the listener's discernment and mission. The finished work was in the load path this week — twice — and that is more than the last three weeks could say.

When the problem a sermon poses is not the problem the cross solves, the cross can only ever be furnishing.

The Editorial

The encouraging reading this week is not the number — it is the proof of possibility. Two pulpits on this watchlist let the finished work carry real weight, and the instrument registered it immediately. The New Covenant does not need a supporting role in the sermon; it needs the load. Righteousness given, standing settled, security resting on God's faithfulness — when those carry the turns of the message, the listener leaves with something that was true before they arrived and will still be true when the week gets hard. The other eight sermons said many true things. The two clearest ones ran on one.

The Index

The Grace Clarity Index (GCI) measures how clearly a sermon presents the finished work of Christ as the foundation of the believer's identity, security, and righteousness. Each sermon is scored on ten items across three dimensions: what it proclaims, what governs its structure, and how it handles the text and the room. The composite is simple arithmetic: two times the sum of the ten items. It does not evaluate sincerity or salvation.

The Index reads every sermon through one lens, and it holds that lens openly. It asks whether the believer's standing was grounded in what Christ finished, or in what the listener was sent away to do. That is a New Covenant standard, and it is not neutral. A preacher from another tradition would not so much fail this index as decline its premise. So a low reading is a diagnosis, not a verdict. It does not measure sincerity, gifting, or whether a life was changed in that room. It measures one current running through the week's teaching, and nothing more.
Calibration check: the Index is periodically run against preachers known for explicit finished-work teaching to confirm it can register clear skies. It can.
